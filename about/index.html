Discretion. Integrity. Diligence.

Discretion

Nothing about a client's business goes further than it needs to. We operate on a strict need-to-know basis. Confidential information stays confidential — before, during, and after an engagement.

Integrity

We tell clients the truth, including when it is not what they want to hear. If the numbers do not stack, we say so. If the business is not ready, we say so. We would rather lose a mandate than give bad advice.

Diligence

We check everything properly before it leaves our hands. Every number. Every assumption. Every claim. The professionals on the other side of the table will — and we ensure there are no surprises.

Drawing on 25 years across every side of the transaction.

Most advisers have worked on one side of the table. We have worked on all sides — as the founder raising capital, the adviser managing the process, the party preparing for sale, and the one conducting due diligence. That breadth changes how we work.

When we review a business ahead of a fundraise, we draw on direct experience of what the investor's team will do when they receive the deck. We know which numbers will be stress-tested, which assumptions will be questioned, and which gaps will stall a deal. We find those things before the investor does.

When we prepare a business for sale, we think like the buyer's advisers. We know what the due diligence process surfaces, how a buyer uses those findings to renegotiate price, and what a clean, well-prepared deal looks like by comparison.

The common thread is preparation. Every engagement begins with an honest assessment of where the client stands and what the other party will conclude when they look at it properly.

When we introduce an opportunity to an investor, we know what that investor expects to receive and in what form. We have placed real estate with family offices and institutional desks. We have introduced growth businesses to investors who have backed them. Those introductions are made after thorough preparation — not before.

Our approach to business.

We take on a small number of clients at any given time. Not because of capacity constraints — we believe good advisory work requires focus. Every client receives direct senior attention throughout. There are no junior-led workstreams. There is no handover once the mandate is agreed.

We begin every engagement with an honest conversation about where the client stands. That sometimes means telling people things they would rather not hear. We think that is more useful than telling them what they want to hear and billing for the privilege.

We do not work with clients we cannot help. If the business is not at a stage where our work will make a material difference, we say so. If a transaction is not viable in its current form, we say so. We would rather pass on a mandate than take it and deliver a poor outcome.

The practice is deliberately small. Fees are structured to reflect the work done, not the outcome of a transaction. We are paid for our work. If the right outcome is to delay, restructure, or not proceed, we say so.

Confidentiality is absolute. Nothing about a client's business is shared beyond the people who need to know it, and then only with the client's explicit knowledge and agreement.
